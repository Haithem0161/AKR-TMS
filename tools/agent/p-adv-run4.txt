Lessons from runs 1 to 3 (the lead rejected or corrected these):
- Read the CHECK or index itself. A constraint may apply to every row, not only to the outcome the text is discussing.
  Quote its full expression before saying it does not cover a case.
- Designed exceptions are not flaws. A behaviour is designed when a DR (docs/plan/research.md) or a stated rule in the
  phase says so (for example, a job acting as the maker and a person as the checker). Grep research.md for the topic
  before reporting; if a DR covers it, report it as GUARDED and cite the DR.
- A sequence that is valid in serial order (each step correct when it commits, and a later step raises a new alert or
  review) is not a race. Say what wrong final state remains.
- Severity follows the outcome, not the mechanism: CRITICAL only when a forbidden decision or filing happens or a true
  detection or a range of source data is lost with nobody told. If the item stays visible to a person (a flag, an open
  alert, a stale banner), it is HIGH at most.
- Before calling something a race, name the two transactions, the rows each locks, and the isolation level (READ
  COMMITTED unless stated).
- Propose the fix from the rule being broken, not from the mechanism you found. Check that your fix actually closes
  the attack (run 3 had a fix that could not reach the case it described) and does not block ordinary work.
- Report GUARDED items in one line each.
