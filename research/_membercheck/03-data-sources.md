# MemberCheck notes 03 — How: data sources and lists
Status: done (2026-10-11)

## Summary

- MemberCheck does not say who compiles its watchlist or PEP data, and no MemberCheck page names a supplier of that data. https://membercheck.com/about-us/ [PRIMARY]
- Its sub-processor list names Acuris Risk Intelligence (England), Kyckr Ireland, Realyou (New Zealand), ASIC and Bing, all under "Data analysis". It does not say what each one supplies. https://membercheck.com/sub-processors/ [PRIMARY]
- The sister brand NameScan (same group, per both About pages) says its Sapphire tier is "powered by the Acuris Risk Intelligence database", with "1.4M+ PEP profiles". That is NameScan's figure. Any link to MemberCheck's data is an inference. https://namescan.io/insights/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- Sanctions named: OFAC, UN, EU and HM Treasury, plus unnamed "national lists and watchlists". The vendor claims over 1,000 sources or lists across 195 countries, refreshed every 24 hours. No Iraqi list is named. https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ and https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- PEPs: FATF Recommendation 12 categories, with relatives and close associates screened in the same check. The FAQ says the database "goes back more than 20 years" (oldest election year). No profile count, tiers or state-owned enterprise treatment. https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ and https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- Adverse media: keyword search over unnamed "reputable news and government sites", grouped by fraud, money laundering, corruption and organised crime. Media languages and Arabic-language media are not stated. https://membercheck.com/faq/ and https://membercheck.com/adverse-media-check/ [PRIMARY]
- Names: fuzzy matching for transliteration and "non-Latin scripts". No page says Arabic-script names are stored, and aliases are not addressed. The software's languages are English, French and Japanese. https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ and https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- Freshness: sanctions changes "In most cases within 24 hours", adverse news "generally added overnight", PEP status changes "usually made within a few days", and "automatic daily screening". https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- Private lists: the platform offers "Custom watchlists and blacklists" that are built inside it. No upload is described. On MemberCheck's pages, the only source a customer can choose is for Australian and New Zealand ID checks. https://membercheck.com/features-sets/ and https://membercheck.com/id-verification/ [PRIMARY]
- ID and business checks: ID covers "60+ countries, expanding to 120+". Company checks cover "130+ countries and states" from "300+" sources, none named. Iraq is listed for ID checks but not for company checks. https://membercheck.com/id-verification/ , https://membercheck.com/knowyourbusiness/ , https://membercheck.com/aml-cft-legislation-in-iraq/ [PRIMARY]

### Q6. Update frequency

- The vendor says its watchlist refresh cycle is 24 hours. Quote: "refreshed on a 24-hour cycle" — https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ [PRIMARY]

Quote: "Re-screen customers automatically as sanctions and PEP lists change, so a cleared customer is reviewed again the moment a new listing affects them." — https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ [PRIMARY]
- [INFERENCE] With a 24-hour refresh cycle, a new designation could take up to about 24 hours, plus processing, to reach screening. Reason: the refresh cycle is the only timing figure on the page, and the page gives no designation-to-listing time. Source: https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ [INFERENCE].
- The rendered date "Last updated 20 August 2026" refers to the web page, not to the data. Quote: "Last updated 20 August 2026" — https://membercheck.com/pep-sanction-screening/ [PRIMARY]

- The FAQ says sanctions changes reach the database "In most cases within 24 hours of the information becoming public", and "typically overnight". Quote: "In most cases within 24 hours of the information becoming public" — https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- The FAQ says adverse-news changes are "generally added overnight". Quote: "New data is generally added overnight." — https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- The FAQ says PEP changes (election, removal from office or resignation) are "usually made within a few days". Quote: "Updates are usually made within a few days of the information becoming public, and in some cases as early as the next day." — https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]
- The FAQ says customers get changes "through automatic daily screening". Quote: "through automatic daily screening." — https://membercheck.com/faq/ [PRIMARY]. The features page says "Real-time detection of changes to your customers' status" (see "Contradictions and surprises").
- [INFERENCE] On the vendor's own figures, PEP status changes arrive more slowly than sanctions changes, about a few days against about one day. Reason: the two FAQ answers give different timings for the two data types. Source: https://membercheck.com/faq/ [INFERENCE].

- The About page says the underlying data "is refreshed daily to keep pace with regulatory change". Quote: "Its underlying data is refreshed daily to keep pace with regulatory change." — https://membercheck.com/about-us/ [PRIMARY]

- The jurisdiction-risk product says "Ratings update as FATF listings, sanctions, and designations change." Quote: "Ratings update as FATF listings, sanctions, and designations change" — https://membercheck.com/jurisdiction-risk/ [PRIMARY]. No time lag is given.
