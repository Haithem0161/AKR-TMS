# 03 — FATF standards: the 40 Recommendations, Immediate Outcomes and guidance

Status: done (2026-10-06). Primary source: the official text "International Standards on Combating Money Laundering
and the Financing of Terrorism & Proliferation — The FATF Recommendations", **updated October 2025** (152 pages),
read from a mirror hosted by the Hong Kong Companies Registry because fatf-gafi.org blocks automated reading.

## 1. What FATF is and why its standards bite an Iraqi bank

- FATF (Financial Action Task Force) is the inter-governmental body that sets the global AML/CFT/CPF standard.
  The 40 Recommendations were adopted in their current form in **February 2012** and amended many times since
  (full history in section 5).
- Countries are assessed in **mutual evaluations** by FATF or by a FATF-style regional body. Iraq belongs to
  **MENAFATF**. Each evaluation rates:
  - **technical compliance** with each of the 40 Recommendations: Compliant, Largely Compliant, Partially
    Compliant, Non-Compliant (C / LC / PC / NC);
  - **effectiveness** against the 11 Immediate Outcomes: High, Substantial, Moderate, Low.
- Poor results put a country under "increased monitoring" (the grey list). Iraq was grey-listed again on
  **19 June 2026** (file 01 section 1). For banks this means foreign correspondents apply enhanced due diligence
  to them (R.19), which raises the cost of every dollar relationship.
- What reaches the bank: the Recommendations bind countries; countries turn them into law and central-bank
  instructions (Iraq: Law 39/2015, CBI Instructions No.1/2023, CBI AML/CFT/CPF controls 2025). Recommendations
  10, 11 and 20 must be set out **in law**, not only in regulation (FATF "note on the legal basis").

## 3. The Recommendations that shape a bank AML system (detail from the text)

### R.10 Customer due diligence (main text)

- **No anonymous accounts** or accounts in obviously fictitious names.
- **When CDD is required:** (i) establishing business relations; (ii) occasional transactions above
  **USD/EUR 15,000**, or payments/value transfers covered by INR.16; (iii) suspicion of ML/TF; (iv) doubts about
  previously obtained identification data. (Iraq applies a stricter USD 10,000 for occasional customers; file 01.)
- **Four CDD measures:**
  - (a) identify and verify the customer with reliable, independent documents, data or information;
  - (b) identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify, including understanding the
    ownership and control structure of legal persons and arrangements;
  - (c) understand the purpose and intended nature of the relationship;
  - (d) **ongoing due diligence and scrutiny of transactions** "to ensure that the transactions being conducted
    are consistent with the institution's knowledge of the customer, their business and risk profile, including,
    where necessary, the source of funds". This sentence is the legal root of transaction monitoring.
- All four apply, but **their extent is risk-based** (INR.1 and INR.10).
- Verification before or during onboarding; may finish after if risk is managed and needed for normal business.
- If CDD cannot be completed: do not open, terminate, and **consider an STR**.
- Existing customers: apply CDD on the basis of materiality and risk.

### R.11 Record-keeping

- Transaction records (domestic and international) for **at least five years**, sufficient to **reconstruct
  individual transactions** (amounts, currency) as evidence for prosecution.
- CDD records, account files, correspondence **and results of any analysis** (e.g. inquiries into complex,
  unusual large transactions) for at least five years after the relationship ends.
- Must be available swiftly to competent authorities.
- System implication: alert dispositions, analyst notes and the data the analyst saw are records in their own
  right; they must be kept and retrievable.

### R.12 Politically exposed persons

- **Foreign PEPs** (as customer or BO): risk-management systems to detect; **senior management approval** to open
  or continue; reasonable measures to establish **source of wealth and source of funds**; **enhanced ongoing
  monitoring**.
- **Domestic PEPs and international-organisation PEPs**: reasonable measures to detect; in higher-risk cases
  apply the three measures above.
- All PEP rules extend to **family members and close associates**.

### R.13 Correspondent banking

- For cross-border correspondent and similar relationships, on top of normal CDD: (a) understand the respondent's
  business, reputation and quality of supervision, including any ML/TF investigation or regulatory action;
  (b) assess its AML/CFT controls; (c) senior-management approval for new relationships; (d) clear split of
  responsibilities; (e) payable-through accounts: respondent has done CDD on customers with direct access and can
  provide it on request.
- **No relationships with shell banks**; make sure respondents do not let shell banks use their accounts.
- Iraqi banks are on both sides: they are respondents of US/European/regional correspondents (who apply R.13 to
  them) and they may act as correspondents for exchange companies and smaller institutions.

### R.15 New technologies

- Identify and assess ML/TF risks of new products, business practices, delivery mechanisms and new or developing
  technologies, **before launch**, and mitigate them.
- VASPs must be regulated, licensed or registered, and supervised.

### R.16 Payment transparency (revised June 2025; title changed from "Wire transfers")

Main text now has three duties:
1. Include **required and accurate originator information** and **required beneficiary information** on payments
   or value transfers and related messages; the information should be **structured to the extent possible** and
   must stay with the payment **throughout the payment chain**.
2. **Monitor** payments for missing originator and/or beneficiary information and take appropriate measures.
3. While processing payments, **freeze and do not deal with designated persons** under UN TF and PF resolutions
   (1267 and successors, 1373, proliferation resolutions).

Detail of the interpretive note (INR.16, June 2025 text with October 2025 clarification):

- **Scope**: cross-border **and domestic** payments, including serial and cover payments. "Originator/beneficiary"
  equal ISO 20022 "debtor/creditor"; "ordering/beneficiary FI" equal "debtor agent/creditor agent". Cash
  withdrawals now count as payments or value transfers (glossary).
- **Structured data**: information should be structured "in accordance with the established standards of the
  system used such as ISO 20022" (para 4). Account numbers must not disguise the country of the servicing FI
  (para 7).
- **Cross-border, at or below the de minimis threshold** (max **USD/EUR 1,000**, not for cash withdrawals): names
  of originator and beneficiary, and account numbers (or a unique transaction reference). Not verified unless
  there is suspicion (para 8).
- **Cross-border above the threshold** (para 9), always:
  - (a) names of originator and beneficiary;
  - (b) account numbers (or unique transaction reference); if funds come from another FI, that FI and account;
  - (c) **originator address** (country and town if no standard postal address) and **beneficiary country and
    town**;
  - (d) **originator date of birth** if a natural person (year of birth if the full date is unknown);
  - (e) for legal persons: connected **BIC**, or **LEI**, or the unique official identifier.
  - Change from the old text: the old menu "address, or national ID number, or customer ID number, or date and
    place of birth" is replaced by address plus date of birth, and legal-entity identifiers are new.
- **Domestic** (paras 10-12): same as cross-border unless the information can be made available by other means,
  in which case an account number or unique reference suffices; the ordering FI must then produce the information
  **within three business days** of a request (law enforcement can compel immediately). (The CBI uses the same
  three-day rule for batch transfers, file 01.)
- **Exceptions** (paras 13-19): FI-to-FI transfers on own behalf need nothing; net settlements need nothing on the
  settlement, but underlying transactions still do; **batch files** from one originator need the originator account
  in each item and full data in the batch; **card purchases** carry the card number, issuer/acquirer data on request;
  card-funded P2P transfers are treated as payments; domestic cash withdrawals need only the account or card number;
  **cross-border card cash withdrawals** carry the card number, and the cardholder name is sent to the acquirer
  within three business days on request.
- **Ordering FI** (paras 20-23): above threshold, required and accurate originator and required beneficiary data;
  below, names and accounts; keep all data under R.11; **must not execute** a non-compliant payment.
- **Intermediary FI** (paras 24-27): keep data with the payment; if technical limits strip it in a domestic leg,
  keep a record for five years; identify payments lacking data, consistent with straight-through processing;
  risk-based policy to **execute, reject or suspend**, and follow-up.
- **Beneficiary FI** (paras 28-31):
  - identify incoming cross-border payments lacking required data; "may include **post-event monitoring or real-time
    monitoring** where feasible" (para 28);
  - above threshold, verify the beneficiary's identity if not done before (para 29);
  - **new: misdirected-payment control** (para 30). Use the beneficiary data in the message to detect misdirected
    payments (ML, fraud, error) with at least one of: (a) per-transaction check that the beneficiary **name and
    account number align** with the bank's records; (b) holistic ongoing monitoring that finds anomalous accounts
    and **misaligned beneficiary information**; or (c) a **confirmation/verification-of-payee** pre-validation
    scheme. Alignment is not exact match; the degree can vary with risk (footnote 60);
  - risk-based policy for execute/reject/suspend for missing data **or potentially misdirected payments**, and
    follow-up (para 31, clarified October 2025 to apply regardless of threshold).
- **MVTS controlling both sides**: consider both sides for the STR decision and file in every affected country
  (para 32). Relevant to Western Union/MoneyGram agents inside Iraqi banks.
- Footnote 49: R.16 does not say **how** to screen payment data against sanctions lists; any effective mechanism
  is acceptable.

What it means for AKR-TMS:
- An **R.16 data-quality rule pack** on incoming and outgoing cross-border payments: missing or meaningless
  originator/beneficiary fields ("NA", "client", dots), missing address/town/country, missing date of birth for
  individuals, missing BIC/LEI/official ID for legal persons, account numbers that hide the country.
- **Name-to-account alignment check** for incoming payments (option (a) or (b) of para 30), using fuzzy Arabic and
  Latin name comparison against the bank's own account-holder names. This doubles as a mule and fraud detector.
- A **repeat-offender view** of correspondents and remitters that keep sending incomplete data (follow-up action
  under paras 27 and 31), feeding the correspondent-bank register.
- From a read-only DB the product can only do post-event monitoring; the phase-2 pre-release API is what lets a
  bank "suspend" before execution.
- The structured ISO 20022 fields (SWIFT CBPR+ since November 2025 for cross-border) should be ingested where the
  bank's payment hub stores them, not only the flattened CBS posting narrative.

### R.17 Reliance on third parties

- Banks may rely on regulated third parties for CDD elements (a)-(c), but **ultimate responsibility stays with the
  bank**; they must get the information immediately and copies of documents without delay on request.

### R.18 Internal controls, foreign branches and subsidiaries

- Banks must run AML/CFT programmes; groups must run **group-wide programmes including information sharing**
  within the group; foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries apply home-country standards.

### R.19 Higher-risk countries

- **EDD** for business relationships and transactions with persons and FIs from countries for which FATF calls
  for it (the "black list" call for action); EDD must be effective and proportionate. Countries apply
  countermeasures when FATF calls for them, or independently.

### R.20 Reporting of suspicious transactions

- If a bank **suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect** proceeds of crime or TF, it must report **promptly**
  to the FIU, by law.

### R.21 Tipping-off and confidentiality

- (a) **Safe harbour**: directors, officers and staff protected from criminal and civil liability for good-faith
  reports, even without knowing the underlying crime and whether or not it happened.
- (b) **Tipping-off prohibited**: no disclosure that an STR or related information is being filed. This must not
  block group information sharing under R.18 (clarified Nov 2017).

### R.1 and INR.1 Risk-based approach (as revised February 2025)

What a bank must do (INR.1 section B):
- **Identify and assess its own ML/TF risks** across four factor groups: customers; countries or geographic areas;
  products, services, transactions; delivery channels. **Document** the assessment, keep it up to date, and be able
  to give it to supervisors. Size it to the nature and size of the business (para 12).
- Policies, controls and procedures to mitigate those risks, **approved by senior management**; monitor that they
  are implemented (para 13).
- **Higher risk -> enhanced measures; lower risk -> simplified measures allowed and encouraged** (paras 14-15).
  Simplified measures are **never** allowed when there is a suspicion of ML/TF (para 2).
- Consider **all relevant risk factors** before deciding the overall risk, and you may **differentiate by factor**:
  e.g. normal CDD at acceptance but enhanced (or simplified) ongoing monitoring, or the reverse (para 16).
- Whatever information is gathered, R.11 retention applies to it.
- **PF risk** (added October 2020): banks must also identify, assess and mitigate **proliferation financing risk**,
  which in FATF terms means strictly the risk of breach, non-implementation or evasion of the **targeted financial
  sanctions of R.7**. This can sit inside the existing sanctions/compliance programme. R.7 itself is **not
  risk-based**: full implementation is mandatory in every risk scenario (paras 3-4, 17-18).
- February 2025 revision: added a focus on **proportionality** ("a proportionate measure is one that appropriately
  corresponds to the level of identified risk and effectively mitigates the risks"), and countries must now
  "**allow and encourage**" simplified measures in lower-risk areas and give guidance on how (para 7). The aim is
  financial inclusion and less de-risking.

What it means for AKR-TMS:
- The product needs a **documented, versioned enterprise-wide risk assessment (EWRA)** with the four factor groups
  plus a separate PF/TFS risk section, signed off by senior management, exportable for the CBI.
- Customer risk scoring must be explainable factor by factor, and controls must be configurable per risk level
  and per factor (e.g. SDD at onboarding but normal monitoring), which supports the CBI rule that SDD is allowed
  below IQD 1m.
- Simplified measures must switch off automatically when there is a suspicion (open alert/case/STR).

### INR.10 Customer due diligence (interpretive note, the detail a system must encode)

- **Suspicion overrides thresholds**: if a bank suspects ML/TF, it should identify and verify the customer and BO
  irrespective of any exemption or threshold, **and file an STR** (para 1).
- **CDD versus tipping-off**: if performing CDD would tip off the customer, the bank may stop the CDD process and
  file the STR instead; staff must be trained to recognise this (paras 2-3). System implication: EDD outreach tasks
  generated while a case is open must warn the user and allow "suppress customer contact".
- **Persons acting on behalf** of the customer must be authorised, identified and verified (para 4): mandates,
  powers of attorney, signatories are part of the CDD data model.
- **Legal persons**: name, legal form, proof of existence; constitutional documents; names of senior managers;
  registered office and principal place of business (para 5a).
- **Beneficial owner cascade for legal persons** (para 5b, footnote 37, measures are cascading, not alternatives):
  1. natural persons with a **controlling ownership interest** (threshold example: **more than 25%**; Iraq uses
     20%, file 01);
  2. if in doubt or nobody controls through ownership: natural persons **controlling by other means**;
  3. if still nobody: the **senior managing official**.
- **Trusts**: settlor, trustee(s), protector, beneficiaries or class, anyone with ultimate effective control;
  "other legal arrangements": persons in equivalent positions (relevant for Iraqi waqf structures).
- Listed companies under disclosure rules, and their majority-owned subsidiaries, are exempt from BO identification.
- **No need to re-verify on every transaction**, unless doubts arise, e.g. suspicion or "a material change in the
  way that the customer's account is operated, which is not consistent with the customer's business profile"
  (para 10). System implication: profile-deviation alerts should be able to trigger a KYC refresh, not only a case.
- **Delayed verification** (non-face-to-face, securities) needs limits on number, type and amount of transactions
  before verification and monitoring of out-of-norm transactions (paras 11-12). System implication: a "pending
  verification" customer state with tighter rules.
- **Existing customers**: CDD on the basis of materiality and risk, at appropriate times (para 13).
- **Higher-risk examples** (para 15):
  - customer: unusual circumstances (unexplained geographic distance), **non-residents**, personal asset-holding
    vehicles, nominee shareholders or bearer shares, **cash-intensive businesses**, unusual or overly complex
    ownership;
  - country: weak AML/CFT per mutual evaluations, under UN sanctions/embargoes, significant corruption or crime,
    funding or hosting terrorism;
  - product/channel: private banking, anonymous transactions (incl. cash), **non-face-to-face without mitigation**,
    payments from unknown or unassociated third parties.
- **Lower-risk examples** (para 17): regulated FIs, listed companies, public administrations; low-premium life
  insurance (annual < USD/EUR 1,000 or single < 2,500); non-surrenderable pensions; **limited financial-inclusion
  products**; countries with effective AML/CFT and low corruption. Regional variation within a country may be
  taken into account (useful for border governorates).
- Lower risk for identification **does not mean** lower risk for ongoing monitoring (para 18).
- **Risk variables** that raise or lower risk: purpose of the account; level of assets or size of transactions;
  regularity or duration of the relationship (para 19).
- **Para 20, the monitoring duty**: examine, as far as reasonably possible, the background and purpose of **all
  complex, unusual large transactions, and all unusual patterns of transactions, which have no apparent economic
  or lawful purpose**. Higher risk -> increase degree and nature of monitoring.
- **EDD examples** (para 20): more customer information (occupation, assets, public databases, internet); more
  frequent updates of customer and BO data; intended nature of the relationship; **source of funds or source of
  wealth**; reasons for transactions; **senior-management approval**; enhanced monitoring with more and better-timed
  controls and selected patterns; first payment through an account in the customer's name at a similarly regulated
  bank.
- **SDD examples** (para 21): verify after onboarding (e.g. when activity passes a threshold); fewer updates;
  reduced monitoring based on a reasonable monetary threshold; infer purpose from the type of account. Never with
  suspicion or specific higher-risk scenarios.
- **Linked transactions**: the USD/EUR 15,000 occasional threshold covers a single operation **or several
  operations that appear to be linked** (para 22). System implication: aggregation across accounts, branches and
  days for occasional customers (Iraq: USD 10,000).
- **Keep CDD data up to date** by reviewing records, particularly for higher-risk customers (para 23): periodic
  review scheduling by risk level (CBI: at least yearly KYC update; risk review every 2 years).

### Interpretive notes to R.12-R.20 (points that matter for a system)

- **INR.12 PEPs and life insurance**: check whether policy beneficiaries (and their BOs) are PEPs at the latest at
  payout; if higher risk, inform senior management before payout, scrutinise the whole relationship, consider an
  STR. (Relevant to bancassurance products sold by Iraqi banks.)
- **INR.13 correspondent banking**: "similar relationships" include those for **securities transactions or funds
  transfers**, as principal or for customers. **Payable-through accounts** = correspondent accounts used directly by
  third parties to transact on their own behalf (the nested-account risk behind the Al-Huda 311 finding, file 01).
- **INR.15 VASPs**: virtual assets count as property/funds; VASP CDD threshold USD/EUR 1,000; the travel rule
  applies to virtual-asset transfers and to **banks sending or receiving virtual-asset transfers for customers**.
  (The CBI 2025 controls ban dealing with unlicensed virtual-asset providers; file 01.)
- **INR.17 reliance**: reliance is not outsourcing. In outsourcing/agency (e.g. agents collecting KYC for the bank)
  the bank's own procedures apply and the bank controls the agent.
- **INR.18 AML/CFT programme contents**, to be sized to risk and size:
  - (a) internal policies, procedures and controls, including **compliance management arrangements** and
    **employee screening at hiring**;
  - (b) **ongoing employee training**;
  - (c) an **independent audit function to test the system**;
  - a **compliance officer at management level**;
  - group programmes: group-level compliance, audit and AML functions receive customer, account and transaction
    information from branches and subsidiaries, **including analysis of unusual activity and possibly STRs**, with
    confidentiality safeguards against tipping-off.
  - foreign operations apply the stricter home standard where host law allows; otherwise extra measures and tell
    the home supervisor.
- **INR.19 countermeasures** a country can order: specific EDD; **systematic reporting of transactions** with the
  country; refuse their branches; limit business relationships; ban reliance on third parties there; **review,
  amend or terminate correspondent relationships** with that country; extra audits. FIs must be told about
  weaknesses in other countries' AML systems. System implication: country lists must drive rules (EDD flag,
  mandatory review, blocking, reporting) with an effective date.
- **INR.20 STRs**:
  - "criminal activity" ideally means **all predicate offences**;
  - TF covers financing of terrorist organisations or individual terrorists **even without a link to a specific
    act**;
  - **all suspicious transactions, including attempted ones, regardless of amount**;
  - the reporting obligation must be **direct and mandatory**.
  - System implication: the case module must handle "attempted" transactions (refused openings, rejected
    payments, abandoned cash deposits) as reportable events, not only posted transactions. In a read-only CBS link,
    attempts are often missing from posted tables: the integration must also read rejected/reversed records and the
    branch observation log.

### R.6 and R.7 Targeted financial sanctions (TFS)

- R.6 (terrorism/TF): freeze **without delay** the funds or other assets of persons designated by the UN 1267/1989
  (ISIL/Al-Qaida) and 1988 (Taliban) committees, and of persons designated nationally under UNSCR 1373; make sure no
  funds are made available to them (INR.6 para 5). Iraq's national list (CFTF/IQTFS, file 01 section 6) is its
  1373 mechanism.
- R.7 (proliferation): the same for UN PF designations. As of the **October 2025** note the resolutions are
  **DPRK** 1718 (2006), 1874, 2087, 2094, 2270, 2321, 2356 and **Iran** 1737 (2006), 1747, 1803, 1929 (2010). The
  Iran resolutions came back into the standard in October 2025 "following re-application of UN Security Council
  Resolutions" (the "snapback" of September 2025). For an Iraqi bank with the Iran border, religious tourism and
  trade flows, this re-activated **UN** (not only US) Iran sanctions screening obligations.
- R.7 TFS obligations are **not risk-based**; FATF has separate non-binding guidance for broader UN measures
  (activity-based prohibitions, vigilance).
- **"Without delay" = ideally within a matter of hours** of a UN designation (glossary). For 1373, upon reasonable
  grounds to suspect. Implication: the CBI rule of list refresh every 12 hours is the outer limit; AKR-TMS should
  pull UN and IQTFS changes several times a day and **re-screen the whole customer base on every list change**
  (delta screening), recording the time from publication to screening.
- **False positives**: countries need publicly known procedures to unfreeze people with the same or similar name
  as a designated person once verified (INR.6 para 9). Implication: a documented false-positive resolution workflow
  with evidence, plus "good guy" whitelisting that is re-checked when the list entry changes.

### Glossary definitions used in system design

- **Beneficial owner** (legal persons): the natural person(s) who ultimately own or control a customer and/or on
  whose behalf a transaction is conducted, including those exercising ultimate effective control. **Only a natural
  person** can be a BO; there can be **several**. "Ultimately" covers chains of ownership and control by means other
  than direct control. Legal arrangements: settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiaries or class, any other person with
  ultimate effective control; if a party is a legal person, look through to its BO.
- **PEPs**: foreign and domestic individuals who **are or have been** entrusted with prominent public functions
  (heads of state or government, senior politicians, senior government, judicial or military officials, senior
  executives of state-owned corporations, important political-party officials). International-organisation PEPs =
  directors, deputies, board members. **Middle-ranking or junior officials are not PEPs** by this definition.
  Iraq context: senior executives of state-owned banks and companies are PEPs; the CBI 2026 circular extends to
  relatives up to the second degree (file 01).
- **Shell bank**: a bank with no physical presence ("meaningful mind and management") in the country where it is
  incorporated and licensed, and not part of a regulated group under consolidated supervision. A local agent or
  low-level staff is not physical presence.

### R.24 and R.25 Beneficial ownership transparency (revised March 2022 and February 2023)

- Countries must use a **multi-pronged approach**: basic information in a **company registry**, BO information held
  by a **public authority or body** (e.g. a BO registry, tax authority, FIU) or an equally efficient alternative,
  plus supplementary sources, which explicitly include **BO information obtained by banks under R.10** (INR.24
  para 7). In other words, the bank's own BO records are part of the national BO system and will be requested.
- BO information must be **adequate, accurate and up to date**; "up to date" means updated within a reasonable
  period, **e.g. within one month** of any change (INR.24 para 9). Countries should consider **discrepancy
  reporting** (banks telling the registry when what they hold differs).
- Registries should give banks timely access at least to basic information, and countries should consider giving
  banks access to BO information (para 11).
- Example BO data fields (footnote 74): full name, nationality(ies), full date and place of birth, residential
  address, national ID number and document type, tax ID.
- **Bearer shares** banned for new issues; existing ones converted or immobilised (para 12). **Nominee shareholders
  and directors** must disclose nominee status and their nominator, or be licensed, or be banned (para 13). When a
  nominee holds control, the BO is the person on whose behalf the nominee acts.
- Foundations, Anstalt, **Waqf** and LLPs get similar treatment (para 14); waqf may instead be legal arrangements
  under R.25, depending on the country.
- R.25 (trusts and similar arrangements): trustees must hold adequate, accurate, up-to-date information on settlor,
  trustees, protector, beneficiaries and anyone with ultimate control, and disclose their status to banks.
- Iraq context: no public BO register exists yet; the MER flags BO transparency as a weakness (file 01). The
  practical source of BO truth for Iraqi banks is their own CDD, so AKR-TMS must make BO capture, verification
  status, ownership percentages, control-by-other-means, nominee flags and the date of last confirmation
  first-class data, and flag BO records older than the review cycle.

## 4. Effectiveness: the 11 Immediate Outcomes and what assessors check inside banks

FATF judges effectiveness through 11 Immediate Outcomes (IOs). Two methodologies matter:

- **2013 methodology (4th round)**, used for Iraq's 2024 MER: IO.3 = supervision, **IO.4 = preventive measures
  (banks and other reporting entities)**. Iraq: IO.4 **Moderate**, IO.11 **Low** (file 01).
- **2022 methodology (5th round, started 2024, text updated through 2025-2026)**: the old IO.3/IO.4 split is
  reorganised by sector. **New IO.3 = supervision and preventive measures for financial institutions and VASPs**;
  new IO.4 = the same for DNFBPs. Other changes: more weight on risk and context, more targeted recommended
  actions with timelines, evaluations about **every 6 years** (instead of roughly a decade), and the countries with
  the lowest scores and highest risks are evaluated first.

The 11 IOs (5th-round numbering):

| IO | Outcome | Where a bank's AML system shows up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Risk, policy and coordination | Bank EWRA consistent with the NRA |
| 2 | International cooperation | Records retrievable for foreign requests |
| 3 | **FIs and VASPs supervised and apply preventive measures commensurate with risk; report suspicious transactions** | The core test of the bank: risk understanding, CDD/EDD, monitoring, STR quality, internal controls |
| 4 | DNFBPs supervised and apply preventive measures | DNFBP customers |
| 5 | Legal persons and arrangements: misuse prevented, BO available | Bank BO data quality |
| 6 | Financial intelligence used by authorities | **STR quality and timeliness**, responses to FIU requests |
| 7 | ML investigated and prosecuted | Evidence packs for law enforcement |
| 8 | Proceeds of crime confiscated | Freeze/seizure execution |
| 9 | TF investigated and prosecuted | TF STRs |
| 10 | TF preventive measures and financial sanctions; NPOs protected | TFS screening and freezing without delay |
| 11 | PF financial sanctions | PF screening, Iran/DPRK exposure, sanctions evasion detection |

Core issues assessors examine for banks (2013 IO.4 core issues, carried into the new IO.3):
1. How well banks **understand their ML/TF risks** and obligations.
2. How well they **apply mitigating measures** commensurate with those risks.
3. How well they apply **CDD and record-keeping** (including BO and ongoing monitoring), and whether they **refuse
   business** when CDD is incomplete.
4. How well they apply **enhanced or specific measures** for (a) PEPs, (b) correspondent banking, (c) new
   technologies, (d) wire-transfer rules, (e) TF targeted financial sanctions, (f) FATF-listed higher-risk countries.
5. How well they meet **reporting obligations** (STRs) and the practical measures to **prevent tipping-off**.
6. How well **internal controls** (including at group level) work, and whether secrecy or other rules impede them.

Why this matters for AKR-TMS: assessors and the CBI ask for **evidence of effectiveness**, not only of rules. The
product should be able to show, per bank and per period: alerts generated vs reviewed vs escalated; STRs filed and
FIU quality feedback; time from list update to screening; time from alert to decision; EDD completion for PEPs and
high-risk customers; refused or exited relationships with reasons; correspondent reviews done; training records.
A one-click "IO.3 evidence pack" and "CBI inspection pack" are strong selling points in a grey-listed market.

### How a country gets grey-listed (ICRG entry criteria, 4th round)

A country enters ICRG review if it is nominated, does not take part in an FSRB, or gets poor MER results, namely any
of: **20 or more NC/PC** technical ratings; **NC/PC on 3 or more of R.3, 5, 10, 11 and 20**; **Low or Moderate on 9
or more IOs with at least 2 Low**; or **Low on 6 or more IOs**. Note that **R.10, R.11 and R.20** (CDD, records,
STRs) are among the five "big" Recommendations: weak bank CDD, record-keeping or reporting directly pushes a
country towards the list.

## 5. FATF guidance that shapes the design of an AML system

### 5a. "Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT" (FATF, July 2021, 76 pages)

Read in full from a Bank of Russia mirror. Main points:
- New technologies "have the potential to make AML/CFT measures **faster, cheaper and more effective**" and support
  financial inclusion (Exec. summary 1).
- AI, machine learning and NLP "can potentially help to better identify risks and respond to, communicate, and
  monitor suspicious activity"; private-sector benefits listed: risk assessments, onboarding, relationship with
  authorities, **auditability, accountability, governance, cost saving** (ES 4).
- The FATF strategic review of the 4th round found that **most jurisdictions and firms still run largely rule-based
  systems**, which "has led to **defensive compliance**" (over-reporting, uniform measures regardless of risk)
  (paras 42-45).
- Private-sector complaint: CDD-driven risk analysis is "**too rule-based rather than behavioural or contextual**",
  which excludes under-served groups (para 90).
- NLP and **fuzzy matching** reduce false positives and false negatives in **sanctions screening** and mainly solve
  data-quality problems by linking information (para 82).
- ML systems "must also improve their degree of **explainability and auditability** in order to fully comply with
  the majority of supervisory requirements"; they stay integrated with human analysis for specific alerts and
  higher-risk areas.
- **Explainability is the key to supervisory acceptance**: "Regulated entities must be able to explain, and remain
  responsible for, the principles and technical details of the innovative solutions before deploying" them;
  supervisors "must be able to understand the models used by AI tools" (para 105).
- **Data quality** was the top obstacle (45% of respondents); legacy systems and the cost of replacing them block
  adoption (paras 108, 119-120).
- **Training data trap**: if training data includes false positives or other errors, "these errors will be
  '**trained-in**' to the machine learning system" (para 125).
- Lack of explainability "can undermine the ability to assess an AI/ML solution's accuracy ... so that its
  effectiveness as an AML/CFT compliance tool cannot be established" (para 131).
- **Human in the loop**: "manual review and human input remains very important"; combining digital efficiency with
  human analysts gives systems that are "fully auditable and accountable" (ES 7).
- Recommended safeguards for new AML solutions: **explainability and transparency of processes and outcomes;
  oversight by humans; privacy and data protection; strong cybersecurity; alignment with standards**; a valid legal
  basis and explicit purpose for processing personal data; privacy-preserving technologies for information
  sharing; good practices for upgrading or replacing legacy systems; technology-neutral, outcomes-based supervision
  (section 5 recommendations).
- Regulators' pilot problem: firms fear criticism if a pilot fails or exposes gaps; the US agencies' December 2018
  joint statement on innovative pilots is cited as a remedy.

What it means for AKR-TMS:
- Run **rules and ML side by side**: CBI-mandated scenarios as transparent rules (always on, mapped to articles), ML
  as a scoring and prioritisation layer and an anomaly detector, never a silent replacement.
- Every model score must show **reason codes** (top contributing factors in plain language, Arabic and English),
  with model documentation, validation reports and versioning available to the CBI and auditors.
- Treat analyst dispositions as training labels only after quality checks (second review, closure reasons), to avoid
  "training in" lazy closures.
- Offer a **"pilot/shadow mode"** where new rules or models run in parallel without creating alerts, with results
  reported, so banks can trial improvements without supervisory risk.
- Data-quality monitoring is a product feature, not an afterthought: completeness and validity dashboards per CBS
  table and field.

### 5b. "Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach: The Banking Sector" (FATF, October 2014, 50 pages)

Read in full from the copy hosted by Saudi Arabia's General Directorate of Financial Investigation.
- The RBA is **"not a 'zero failure' approach"**: a bank that took all reasonable measures can still be misused
  (para 10). Low risk still needs mitigation (para 11).
- **Bank risk assessment** (paras 54-58): documented, maintained, communicated; proportionate to size and complexity
  (simple for small banks, sophisticated for multi-branch, multi-product banks); factors include business nature
  and scale, target markets, **number of customers already rated high risk**, jurisdictions exposed to (corruption,
  organised crime, FATF-listed), distribution channels and reliance on third parties, **internal audit and
  regulatory findings**, volume and size of transactions. Inputs: heads of business, relationship managers, the
  NRA, official lists, mutual evaluations, typologies. Review periodically and when circumstances or threats change.
  **Approved by senior management**, states the **risk appetite** and the acceptable risk level.
- Risk by banking activity (Box 2): retail (cash-intensive businesses, volume, high value); wealth management
  (confidentiality, BO opacity, offshore trusts, PEPs); investment banking (layering); **correspondent banking (high
  value, limited remitter and source-of-funds information, respondents in weak jurisdictions, PEP ownership of
  banks)**.
- **Customer risk profile** from a holistic view of CDD data drives the level and type of ongoing monitoring and
  the accept/continue/exit decision; profiles can be built for **homogeneous customer groups** (similar income,
  similar transactions), "particularly relevant for retail banking" (para 60). This legitimises **peer-group /
  segment baselines** in AKR-TMS.
- **Ongoing monitoring** (paras 66-70):
  - scrutiny of transactions for consistency with the bank's knowledge of the customer **and the nature and purpose
    of the product**; also detecting **changes in the customer profile** and keeping it up to date;
  - continuous or triggered by specific transactions; may **compare a customer's activity with a peer group**;
  - for high volumes "**automated systems may be the only realistic method**", but banks must "**understand their
    operating rules, verify their integrity on a regular basis and check that they address the identified ML/TF
    risks**" (para 67);
  - depth of monitoring follows the institutional risk assessment and customer risk; adequacy of the monitoring
    system and the factors behind adjustments **reviewed regularly** (para 68);
  - **document the criteria and parameters** for customer segmentation and risk levels, and make the criteria for
    monitoring frequency and intensity per segment **transparent** (para 69);
  - examples (Box 5): high risk = daily monitoring, manual review, frequent analysis, destination of funds, red flags
    from typology reports, reporting to senior management; lower risk = thresholds, low frequency, automated
    systems;
  - Basel (BCBS) adds that for most banks, especially internationally active ones, effective monitoring "is likely
    to necessitate the automation of the monitoring process";
  - **document, retain and communicate monitoring results** and the queries raised and resolved (para 70).
- **Reporting** (paras 71-72): banks need the ability to flag unusual movements for analysis and "**appropriate case
  management systems** so that such funds or transactions are scrutinised in a timely manner"; once suspicion has
  formed, report promptly.
- **Internal controls and governance** (paras 73-85): clear allocation of AML responsibility; **controls to monitor
  staff integrity**; group-wide consistency; senior management and board own the risk appetite and refuse
  relationships with unmitigable risk; a skilled **compliance officer at management level** with independence,
  authority, resources and **access to all internal information** across lines of business and branches; staff
  vetting proportionate to the ML/TF exposure of each role (not only senior staff); **training** that is
  high-quality, mandatory, tailored per business line, **tested for effectiveness**, ongoing; controls monitored
  continuously by compliance and reviewed by **audit**.

What it means for AKR-TMS:
- Built-in **"tuning and validation" evidence**: rule logic visible in plain language, periodic rule-effectiveness
  reports (alerts, true-positive rate, STR conversion per rule), sign-off history. This answers "understand their
  operating rules, verify their integrity regularly".
- **Segment and peer-group baselines** (salary earners, traders, students, NGOs, government employees) as a
  standard monitoring dimension, with the segmentation criteria documented in the product.
- Risk-tiered monitoring profiles (e.g. high-risk customers reviewed daily, with lower thresholds).
- Case management with timeliness tracking (age of alerts, SLA breaches) because "timely scrutiny" is a test.
- Training and staff-integrity modules are adjacent features (training records are also a CBI record type).

### 5c. Other FATF guidance relevant to the product

Items marked (verified) were confirmed from the document or a secondary source in this session; the others are
summaries of well-known FATF guidance not re-read here.

- **R.16 revision timing (verified)**: the revised R.16 ("Payment transparency") was published **18 June 2025**;
  expected implementation **by the end of 2030 at the latest**; FATF consulted on accompanying R.16 guidance,
  including how to use the three alignment-check options for misdirected payments. AKR-TMS can sell "R.16-2030
  readiness" (data-completeness and name-alignment checks) years before the deadline.
- **Guidance on Digital Identity (6 March 2020, verified)**: non-face-to-face CDD that relies on **reliable,
  independent digital ID systems** with appropriate **assurance levels** "may present only a standard level of risk,
  or may even be lower-risk"; the assurance level used should match the customer's risk (lower assurance can do for
  SDD, high-risk customers need high assurance); governments should audit/certify digital ID systems. Relevant to
  Iraq's biometric National Card and the CBI digital-bank licences: AKR-TMS should record **how** each customer was
  identified (in branch, National Card chip/biometric check, remote video, agent) as a risk factor and as evidence.
- **Guidance on PEPs (Recommendations 12 and 22), June 2013**: PEP status is preventive, not an accusation; sources
  for detection include commercial databases, internet and media, customer declarations, **asset-disclosure
  registers**, government lists; treatment of former PEPs should be **risk-based, not a fixed time limit**; red flags
  include use of corporate vehicles or relatives to hide ownership, unexplained wealth versus declared salary, funds
  moving to or from government accounts, and transactions in high-corruption countries.
  For Iraq: the Federal Commission of Integrity's asset declarations and published lists of senior officials are
  natural data sources for a local PEP register.
- **Correspondent Banking Services guidance, October 2016**: the FATF Recommendations **do not require
  correspondents to do CDD on each customer of the respondent** ("no KYCC"); they assess the respondent's controls
  and may send requests for information about specific transactions; wholesale termination of relationships
  (de-risking) is not what FATF expects. Iraqi banks face exactly these RFIs from US and regional correspondents; the
  product should answer them fast with a transaction-and-customer evidence pack.
- **Guidance on Proliferation Financing Risk Assessment and Mitigation, June 2021**: how banks assess the risk of
  breach, non-implementation or evasion of PF TFS; typical evasion via **front and shell companies, intermediaries,
  shipping (ship-to-ship transfers, flag changes), dual-use goods, trade finance and correspondent accounts**. The
  CBI 2025 controls chapter 9 PF indicators follow the same structure (file 01).
- **FATF/Egmont Trade-Based Money Laundering: Trends and Developments (December 2020) and TBML Risk Indicators
  (March 2021)**: indicators in structural, trade-activity, trade-document and commodity, account and transaction
  groups; schemes include over- and under-invoicing, multiple invoicing, phantom shipments, misdescribed goods,
  front companies and hawala-settled trade. Directly relevant to Iraq's import-invoice and dollar-purchase abuse
  (file 02).
- **Financing of the Terrorist Organisation ISIL (February 2015)** and **TF risk indicators (2016)**: ISIL revenue
  from occupied territory (bank looting, extortion, oil, antiquities), kidnapping for ransom, donations incl. via
  NPOs, foreign terrorist fighters' support networks. Base material for an Iraq TF scenario pack.
- **Beneficial ownership guidance**: legal persons (March 2023) and legal arrangements (March 2024), supporting
  the 2022/2023 R.24/R.25 revisions (multi-pronged approach, registries, discrepancy reporting).
- **Data pooling stocktake (July 2021)** and **"Partnering in the fight against financial crime: data protection,
  technology and private sector information sharing" (July 2022)**: privacy-enhancing technologies (homomorphic
  encryption, secure multi-party computation, federated learning, zero-knowledge proofs) can let institutions
  share risk signals while respecting data protection. Relevant to a multi-bank product: a future cross-bank
  "shared signals" service would need an explicit legal basis from the CBI/AML Office (to research in file 21/30).
- **Unintended consequences stocktake (October 2021)** and the **February 2025 R.1/INR.1/INR.10 revision**:
  de-risking, financial exclusion and undue targeting of NPOs are failures of implementation; proportionate,
  risk-based measures are the expected answer.
